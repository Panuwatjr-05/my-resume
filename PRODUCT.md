# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, recruiters, and senior engineers reviewing candidates for software and web development roles. They scan quickly, decide in seconds whether to read further, and are attuned to quality signals — sloppy design reads as sloppy engineering.

## Product Purpose

A personal resume and portfolio site for a software/web developer. Its job is to make one argument clearly: this person is precise, capable, and worth a conversation. Success means a recruiter or hiring manager reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, sophisticated. The voice is confident without being loud. Every word earns its place. The tone is that of someone who knows their craft and doesn't need to prove it with noise.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS portfolio templates: hero-metric layouts, gradient text, identical card grids, glassmorphism. These read as AI-generated and signal a lack of taste.
- Anything that could belong to a product company's marketing site rather than a person.
- Overcrowded layouts where information density signals effort rather than clarity.

## Design Principles

1. **Signal through restraint** — every element must justify its presence. If removing it doesn't hurt the argument, remove it.
2. **Typography as hierarchy** — weight, size, and spacing carry the structure. Color is used sparingly and purposefully, not decoratively.
3. **Technical confidence** — the design itself demonstrates engineering sensibility: precise alignment, consistent rhythm, nothing accidental.
4. **Readable first** — the reader is skimming. Structure content to reward both the 10-second skim and the careful read.
5. **No performance** — avoid patterns that exist to impress rather than inform. The portfolio is evidence, not a pitch deck.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. Reduced motion respected — animations (if any) must have a static fallback. Semantic HTML structure for screen reader compatibility.
